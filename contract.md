## Contract Questions

1. When and where will we schedule meetings (e.g. 11am Wednesdays and 
   2pm Fridays: in-person at X location, or on Teams video call)?
   We will meet every Thursday at 2:00 PM in-person. Additional meetings may be arranged as needed, particularly approaching iteration deadlines.


2. How will meeting hosts be planned so that team members share the 
   leading of meetings equally?
   Meeting leadership will rotate among team members based on who is leading the relevant section of work that week. This ensures the workload and responsibility remain evenly distributed.


3. Where will we record our meeting minutes (Gitlab Wiki or Gitlab 
   markdown file)?
   Minutes will be recorded in the GitLab Wiki after each meeting, and all members are expected to review them within 24 hours.


4. How many days before the iteration due date do we expect individual 
   tasks to be completed by (i.e. when is our internal deadline(s))? 
   > The course suggests 5-7 days for groups aiming for high marks
   > to make full use of Preview runs and tutor feedback
   All individual tasks must be completed 7 days before the iteration due date. This allows time to incorporate feedback and any tutor suggestions before final submission.



5. What is the expected response time for messages/posts on MS Teams 
   when communicating with each other?
   > The course suggests 24-48 hours, or 12 hours within 5 days of a deadline
   All team members are expected to respond to messages on MS Teams within 24 hours. Within 5 days of a deadline, responses are expected within 12 hours.


6. What is the expected turn-around time for reviewing merge requests? 
   What is our approach to conducting code reviews?
   > The course suggests 24-48 hours, or 12 hours within 5 days of a deadline
    Merge requests should be reviewed and approved within 48 hours of submission. Within 5 days of a deadline, this is reduced to 12 hours. Reviewers should check for correctnes and clarity to team coding standards before approving.



7. How will we handle conflicts (i.e. differing opinions)? Note: If 
   conflicts cannot resolved this way, please contact your tutor.
   > Include a timeframe & different strategies like Teams messaging
   > group meetings, contacting your tutor
  When disagreements arise, the team will:
   1. Allow each member to present their perspective
   2. List the pros and cons of each proposed approach
   3. Reach a collective decision, prioritising what best serves the project
   Conflicts should be resolved within 24 hours. If a resolution cannot be reached, the team will escalate to the course tutor.

8. List the steps a team member does if they get stuck (e.g. stuck 
   debugging).
   > e.g. team member assistance, tutor/lab time help, Forum, Help sessions
   If a team member is stuck on a task:
      1. Attempt to solve the problem independently for a reasonable amount of time
      2. Ask teammates for help via MS Teams or at the next meeting
      3. Search online resources and course materials
      4. Attend tutor/lab help sessions or post on the course forum
      5. If still unresolved, contact the tutor directly



9. A day before the due date we notice a team member's work is very 
   broken and incomplete. Instead of waiting until the due date, list 
   the steps we will take to prevent this from occuring in the first 
   place. Note: contact your tutor if this happens as well. 
   To avoid last-minute issues with incomplete work:
   1. Tasks are broken into smaller, trackable pieces using the GitLab issue board
   2. Internal deadlines give the team time to identify and address gaps early
   3. Team members should communicate if they are falling behind
   4. If a member's work is at risk, the team will reassign to cover the gap and notify the tutor if necessary
